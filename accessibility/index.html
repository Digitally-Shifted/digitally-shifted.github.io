Accessibility

A site that works for everyone.

We build this website to the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2, level AA, and test it with automated checks and by hand. Where a part falls short of AA, we name it on this page.

What the site supports

  • Every page works with a keyboard. Focus is always visible as a 2px orange ring.
  • Pages use headings, landmarks and labels that screen readers can follow, and a skip link goes straight to the content.
  • Text, links, buttons and tick boxes meet the WCAG AA contrast ratios. Links are always underlined, never signalled by colour alone.
  • The outlines of the text fields and selects on the forms use the brand's darker grey. That outline measures 1.71 to 1 against the dark panel around the fields, below the 3 to 1 ratio WCAG 1.4.11 asks for a control boundary. We keep this colour by owner decision of 2 November 2026 and record it here. Field labels, text contrast and focus rings meet AA in full.
  • Zoomed to 400%, or on a screen 320 pixels wide, pages reflow into one column without sideways scrolling.
  • If your device asks for reduced motion, the site drops the moving and scroll-driven effects and keeps simple fades.
  • Anything that moves on its own, such as the scrolling client logos, can be stopped with the Pause motion button. This browser remembers the choice.
  • High-contrast and forced-colour settings (such as Windows Contrast themes) keep buttons, panels and markers visible.
  • The 3D visuals are decoration. Everything they show is also written in text on the page.
  • If your browser asks to save data, or the connection is slow, the 3D visuals are not downloaded at all.
  • Forms have visible labels, and errors explain how to fix them.
  • The quote form runs a bot check (Google reCAPTCHA). When it is switched on, Google's badge sits over the bottom-right corner of the page, and Google's frames add keyboard stops that do not carry our focus ring. Scrolling moves any focused item out from under the badge; every control the site builds itself keeps the rules on this page.
  • The Chain Check tells screen readers how many links are in place, and which one to fix first, after each change.

How we check it

Each release is tested with axe-core against WCAG 2.2 A and AA rules on every page, at desktop and mobile sizes, together with keyboard, reduced-motion, text-spacing and 320-pixel reflow checks.
